Question 03 · Test the inference

Does progress on a test transfer to work?

Sometimes. The useful question is which task, for which people, under which conditions. Inspect what the AI contributed and what people still had to do.

Why some claims were excluded

Source quality, task definition, and the scope of a claim matter. These examples explain where a stronger conclusion would outrun the evidence.

    Illustrative only · Not measured AI data

    Two points do not choose your forecast.

    Imagine a score rose from 20 to 30 in one year. Three rules fit both points exactly. Move forward in time to see their different implications.

    This invented score runs from 0 to 100. Values above 100 expose an assumption that breaks at a ceiling; they are not clipped or presented as possible scores.

    Make the claim specific.

    “This system improved on these tasks, under these conditions” is a useful starting point. To plan a change in your own work, choose a representative task and measure quality, time, cost, and failures against your current approach.

    Faster benchmark progress can coexist with slower work in a particular setting. Neither result decides the other. Future performance depends on assumptions that should be stated and tested.
