Every AI model since ChatGPT

Those five tests were run on the AI models below. The chart plots every significant one released since ChatGPT arrived in late 2022 — each dot is one model, placed by the date it came out and coloured by the company that made it. Drag the slider, or press Play, to watch them arrive over time.

A hollow ring is a model you can download and run on your own computer; a solid dot is one you can only use through a company’s website. Height is a rough sense of how advanced a model was for its moment — deliberately not an exact score, because no single number can fairly rank a hundred models across four years and twenty companies. That over-simplification is one of the things this demo argues against.

What AI has actually done

A test score is only a stand-in for the real question: has any of this made a difference outside of an exam? Here is what AI has actually done in six fields where the results can be checked — proteins, medicine, weather, materials, mathematics and software. Some of it earned a Nobel Prize. Some of it turned out to be a compound first reported in 1972.

Each result below separates what the machine did from what the people around it did, because that gap is where almost all of the overclaiming happens.

    —months

    How far the best downloadable models trail the best paid ones

    The five tests you drew came from the same handful of AI models, over the same few years. Most of them ended up further along than people tend to guess. The last one climbed to nearly solved — and then, on a harder version of the same test, dropped back to almost nothing.

    And the most careful study anyone has run on these tools found experienced programmers working 19% slower with them, while being sure they were working faster.

    So the honest conclusion is neither “AI is racing ahead” nor “AI is overhyped”. Both are visible in the same data, in the same months. It is that “how good is AI” is not one number that rises together: the edge of what it can do is jagged, and the only reliable way to know which side of that edge a particular job falls on is to try the job and measure it. Intuition will not tell you — including the intuition you just tested over five rounds.
