    How to play

    Three things people call AI

    Tic-tac-toe against three different opponents. They are numbered along the top, and they are not three difficulty levels. They compare written instructions, experience stored in a table, and a neural network that learns to approximate that experience.

    1. 1a · Rules-based intelligence. Eight if/else rules a person wrote down. It has played zero games and it cannot be beaten — the app searches every game that could still be played against it and finds no line where it loses. Every rule it uses is listed on the screen, and it tells you which one it just used and why. Turn rules off with First 2 / First 4 / All 8: two rules is what most people write first, and the app will show you the game where that loses.
    2. 1b · Machine learning. The move scores begin at zero. People supplied the game representation, rewards, and learning procedure; experience changes the scores. Try the untrained opponent, then press TRAIN — it plays 5,000 games against itself and against chance and freezes the result as a new era. Play the new era. Train again. Somewhere around the third to fifth burst the same exhaustive search stops finding a line where it loses.
    3. 1c · Neural networks. First choose Create learning examples. The fresh copy of the 1b algorithm plays 20,000 seeded games and its current estimates are frozen as examples. Then train a smaller network with shared weights. The screen keeps some board positions out of training and shows those results separately from its actual play. Its moves come from those weights, not a hidden table lookup.

    The full rule set and a sufficiently trained table can both play unbeatable tic-tac-toe. People wrote the first opponent's move priorities directly; the second learns its priorities through games. The same exhaustive search checks each displayed policy before the app calls it unbeatable.

    Show its brain (steps 1b and 1c) prints the value it has learned or predicted for every empty square — gold looks promising to it, red-orange looks dangerous. Era 0 is all zeros because it genuinely knows nothing. The dropdown lets you go back and play any earlier era.

    Everything happens on this device: no network, no account, nothing stored. Reloading starts over. Reopen these instructions any time with the ? button at the top.
