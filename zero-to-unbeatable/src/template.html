How to play

Beat it · teach it · fail to beat it

You are playing tic-tac-toe against an opponent that has never played a game. No strategy, no rules about rows, no opening book — just a list of numbers, every one of them zero.

  1. Beat Era 0. Tap a square. It replies at random, because every square looks identical to it. You should win, and win fast.
  2. Press TRAIN. It plays 5,000 games against itself and against chance, keeps what worked, and freezes the result as a new era.
  3. Play the new era. Same program, same code — only the numbers changed. It blocks you now.
  4. Train again, and again. Somewhere around the third to fifth burst the app searches every game that can still be played against it, finds no line where it loses, and says so. That banner is a proof, not a win record.

Show its brain prints the value it has learned for every empty square — gold looks promising to it, red-orange looks dangerous. Era 0 is all zeros because it genuinely knows nothing. The dropdown lets you go back and play any earlier era, and that comparison is the whole lesson.

Nine at once, from the toggle at the top, drops the same learner into a world one step bigger — nine boards, first to win five.

Everything happens on this device: no network, no account, nothing stored. Reloading returns it to Era 0. Reopen these instructions any time with the ? button at the top.

At a glance

Before you start

Presenter mode on if you are projecting. To reproduce a rehearsed run, type your seed into Rehearsal seed and press Reset to Era 0. Leave the burst on 5,000. Do not train before the session, and do not refresh mid-demo.

The 15 minutes

  • 0:00 Hand it to a student: “Beat it.” Let two or three people beat Era 0. Let the room laugh at it.
  • 2:00 Show its brain — every square +0.00, seen 0 times. That is the whole mind.
  • 3:00 Press Train and say nothing for three seconds. Point at ε falling and the win rate climbing.
  • 4:30 Play Era 1. It blocks. Still beatable about one game in six — ask what it is missing.
  • 6:30 Train, play Era 2. Wins dry up, draws start. Let the frustration land; it is the lesson.
  • 9:00 Train twice more. The banner lands between the third and fifth press. Invite the best player to try.
  • 11:30 Go back to Era 0 and stomp it again. Same code, different numbers.
  • 13:00 Read the banner, then bridge: tic-tac-toe can be listed, chess and language cannot.

Second act, 5 minutes

Switch to Nine at once and play — beatable again, nothing taken away. Turn the brain on and read the last line (most squares sit on pictures it has never seen). Train, then read how often a position comes round: about ten times on one board, about once on nine. Close on the missing banner — here the app prints a refusal instead of a proof.

If it goes sideways

  • Short of time? Press train twice in a row and skip Era 2. It always lands by the fifth press.
  • A student beats Era 3? They cannot beat a verified era, but they can beat one before the banner. Say so, then train again.
  • “It was secretly programmed.” Show the brain at Era 0, then run Settings → the full self-test in front of them.
