## Scientific and teaching contract

Use a few authored event histories, not procedural geology or a basin simulator.
An event ledger deposits sediment at stated rate, pauses deposition, erodes the
newest surviving material, and resumes deposition. Elapsed time and rock thickness
are distinct quantities with visible units. Prefer model time in Myr since start
(define a million years) and thickness in metres; do not quietly mix elapsed time
with ages before present. Every layer, gap and fossil must trace to the ledger.

Linked preserved-rock and elapsed-time views make missing intervals visible;
a second section may preserve evidence missing from the first. Known synthetic
species lifetimes are filtered by preservation and sampling. An unsampled layer
is not an observed fossil absence. Preserve real specimen positions in surviving
rock; erosion removes specimens in removed deposits. Do not map destroyed fossils
onto hiatus boundaries. No reworking in first version. Partial-layer erosion and
constant-deposition time/depth mapping must have explicit, tested behavior.

Three compact authored cases can cover an actual end of a simulated species'
lifetime, missing strata, and inadequate sampling. Do not reveal scenario identity
or true lifetimes in student labels before the evidence activity. Students make
a prediction, inspect/sample a layer or compare a second section, identify what
changed in evidence, then choose a warranted conclusion. Showing gaps must be an
observable action with meaning, not a decorative animation. Color/pattern layers
and fossil marks coherently, with legible axes on mobile.

Score conclusions supported by VISIBLE evidence, not a guess of the one hidden
history. A local disappearance need not be extinction, a hiatus need not involve
erosion, and equal thickness need not mean equal duration. Two local sections
cannot certify global extinction. Ground-truth reveal explains this authored
synthetic history without upgrading sparse observations into proof. Include an
explicitly justified unresolved conclusion and a new-case transfer check. Avoid
making every answer 'uncertain' by showing concrete evidence that rules out some
explanations while others remain possible.

Primary conceptual references: StratPal event-data tutorial and author workshop,
linked in the reviewed Fellow artifact. Their occurrence-event transformation is
not itself an algorithm for preserving actual fossil specimens. Verify relevant
primary-source content. Keep boundaries next to the relevant inference/result.

Independent checks: hand-worked deposit/pause/erode/resume stacks; top-down erosion
across multiple layers; partial-layer erosion; eroded fossils absent; surviving
specimens within layer and species lifetime bounds; constant-deposition linear
mapping; hiatus duration distinct from thickness; sample reveal only changes
observations, not truth. Test evidence/feedback against displayed observations,
including same observations compatible with more than one history. Production
module is bundled verbatim into the shipped page. Persist runnable checkpoints.
