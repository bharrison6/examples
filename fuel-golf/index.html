🎤 Fuel Golf — stage notes

Everything you need in front of a class, in one screen. The full write-up is the teacher guide.

Run of show — 20 minutes

0–3 minProject the game (⚙ → Presentation mode). Fly Level 1 together: time-warp, the burn planner, the dotted preview. Vocabulary: periapsis (low, fast), apoapsis (high, slow), prograde.
3–8 minStudents play Levels 1–2 in pairs. Have them open the Physics HUD and watch "energy gained per 1 Δv" as the ship swings around.
8–15 minThe main event: Level 3, "Escape Artist." Par 791 m/s, tank 2,057 — escaping from apoapsis costs ≈1,835 m/s, from periapsis ≈661. Let them discover it. First to make par explains where they burned and what the HUD showed.
15–19 minDebrief on the projector with a student's energy-vs-time plot and the "show the math" panel under it — it evaluates Δε = v·Δv + ½Δv² with that student's own numbers and checks it against the simulation. The burn is a vertical jump in ε, taller when v was larger, for the same Δv.
19–20 minPost the leaderboard. Levels 4–7 (Hohmann, powered flyby, finite thrust) make good homework or a second session.

The one idea

ΔKE = v·Δv + ½Δv². The v·Δv term dominates: the same Δv buys energy in proportion to how fast you already are. Speed peaks at periapsis, so a burn there buys the most orbital energy ε = ½v² − μ/r. Potential is unchanged at the instant of the burn — only the KE jump differs. The HUD's "energy per unit Δv" readout is v.
If someone asks where the extra energy comes from: the fuel's energy is fixed, but energy delivered to the ship is force × distance. At high speed the engine acts over a longer distance per second, so more of the propellant's energy ends up in the ship and less in the exhaust left behind.

Catch these three

1."It's better at periapsis because you're closer." Proximity is not the mechanism — speed is. The formula contains v, not r. Being deep in the well matters only because falling there is what made the ship fast.
2."Oberth is the same as a gravity assist." An unpowered assist (Level 5's moon) steals energy from the moon's orbital motion — no fuel. An Oberth burn spends fuel, just at the most profitable moment. A powered flyby is both.
3."Δv costs the same anywhere." Δv spent is the same anywhere; the energy it buys is not. That is the whole game.

Numbers to have ready

Pars (m/s): L1 712 · L2 1,107 · L3 791 · L4 1,819 · L5 1,424 · L6 838 · L7 823.
Level 3 escape: ≈661 m/s from periapsis vs ≈1,835 m/s from apoapsis.
Level 6 (0.055 m/s²): lit at Pe 919 m/s, burn centered on Pe 775.
Level 7 (0.013 m/s²): one long burn 1,498 m/s, six ~2.8 h perigee kicks 731 — which is why real upper stages escape via repeated perigee kicks.
The planet is Earth at true scale: μ = 398,600 km³/s², R = 6,371 km. The moon is fictional (closer and heavier than Luna) so an assist fits one class.

Ask the room

  1. The plot shows a taller jump in ε for the same Δv at periapsis — which term of ΔKE = v·Δv + ½Δv² explains it?
  2. The HUD says "energy per unit Δv = current speed." Differentiate ½v² to show why.
  3. Why does a prograde burn at periapsis raise the apoapsis, on the far side of the orbit?
  4. In Level 5, where does the escape energy come from when you fly close behind the moon without burning? What slows down in exchange?
  5. Could you escape with a radial-out burn? Try it — why does pushing "straight up" waste fuel?

Logistics

Leaderboards persist per level on each machine — reset them between class periods in ⚙ Settings. For Levels 6–7, point students at the HUD's time-to-periapsis, the planner's burn duration, and the lead box beside → Pe: those three let them compute an ignition point instead of guessing. Keys: Space pause · B burn · Enter commit · Z undo · X cut engine · P/A warp to Pe/Ap · H HUD · M briefing · R restart. Undo rewinds the last burn (also offered after a crash) — students should iterate, not restart.

⛳ Fuel Golf — how to play

You have a spacecraft, a tank of Δv, and a mission. Lowest Δv wins.

Your first two minutes

1. Read the mission — every level names a target and a par Δv budget, the fuel a skilled pilot needs. Your tank is bigger than par, so you can afford to experiment. 2. Time-warp to a good spot (→ Pe or → Ap). 3. Hit 🔥 Plan burn, aim, drag the Δv slider, and read the dotted preview before you spend anything. 4. Commit. Wrong call? ↶ Undo puts the fuel back. Nothing is ever wasted by trying.

Tap anywhere outside this box, press Esc, or hit Fly to close it — ❓ in the top bar brings it back any time.

Flying

Time-warp with ⏸ / 1× / 10× / 50× / 200×, or jump straight to periapsis (→ Pe, the low fast point) or apoapsis (→ Ap, the high slow point). Set a lead in minutes to stop short of the apsis — essential once your engine takes real time to burn. Drag the map to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, ⊕ Follow keeps the camera on your craft.

Burning

Hit 🔥 Plan burn (or B). Pick a direction — prograde, retrograde, radial, or free angle (drag the map to aim) — and set Δv. The dotted line previews your new orbit, with hollow Pe/Ap markers showing where the new apsides land, before you spend fuel. Misjudged it? ↶ Undo rewinds the last burn.

The lesson hiding in the game

Adding Δv changes your kinetic energy by ΔKE = v·Δv + ½Δv². The v·Δv term means the same burn buys more energy when you're already moving fast — and you're fastest at periapsis. That's the Oberth effect. Open the Physics HUD and watch "energy per unit Δv" climb as you fall toward periapsis.

Real numbers

The planet is Earth at true scale: μ = 398,600 km³/s², radius 6,371 km. Speeds are km/s, Δv budgets m/s, altitudes km, and orbital periods run hours to days — which is why time-warp exists. The moon is fictional (closer and heavier than our Moon) so a gravity assist fits a class period.
