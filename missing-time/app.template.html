A bounded rock-record investigation

Inference boundaries

  • A local last observation is not a global extinction claim.
  • A hiatus can form without erosion.
  • Equal rock thickness does not mean equal elapsed time.
  • Two local sections cannot certify global extinction.

This is a small authored preservation model, not a basin simulator. It has no reworking, fossil transport, dating uncertainty, or global distribution model.

How to investigate missing time

  1. Choose a numbered investigation. Its hidden authored history is not named before reveal.
  2. Sample one or more surviving layers. A sample can show specimens, or no observed specimens; neither changes the rock record.
  3. Select Show supplied age evidence. The exact Myr span comes from explicitly labelled synthetic chronology anchors bracketing the rock, never from thickness alone.
  4. Use only the visible evidence to select a conclusion. Then reveal the authored history and compare it with what was warranted.

Keep quantities separate: Myr since start is elapsed model time; metres are surviving rock thickness.

Presenter’s notes

Ask students what observation could distinguish a local disappearance from extinction. Do not let the column height substitute for time evidence: have them name the chronology anchors before interpreting the gap.

For Case 2, point out that erosion removes material from the top down, including any specimens in removed deposits. For Case 3, ask what the second local section changes and what it still cannot prove.
