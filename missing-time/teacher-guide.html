Missing Time

Teacher and presenter guide · rock records, preservation, and warranted inference · Murray State University

Purpose

Students use a compact authored rock record to keep three claims separate: elapsed time, surviving rock thickness, and fossil observations. They learn that preservation and sampling constrain what a local disappearance can mean.

Units. Model time is Myr since start, where one Myr means one million years. Rock height is in metres (m). Thickness never supplies a time duration by itself.

Core flow (12–15 minutes)

  1. Open index.html; the how-to appears automatically. Use ? to reopen it.
  2. In Case 1, sample the Asteria-bearing middle rock and younger top rock. Use the chronology action to confirm continuity. Ask why a local last observation still is not extinction evidence.
  3. In Case 2, show the anchor-bracketed gap and sample surviving rock below and above it. Require students to state what the visible evidence says before revealing the authored mechanism.
  4. In Case 3, sample below Section C’s gap and then Green mudstone in Section D. Ask what D changes about C’s blank.
  5. After a warranted conclusion, reveal the authored history. Treat the reveal as a model explanation, not proof students could have made from sparse evidence.

Chronology evidence

The action labelled Show supplied age evidence displays synthetic anchors at edges of preserved rock for every relevant section. Those explicit inputs supply an exact Myr interval when one is missing, or establish continuity when none is missing. A clickable boundary or a layer’s height does not.

Observation rule. Sampling makes an observation visible; it does not alter the event ledger. A layer with no observed specimen is not an observed fossil absence unless it has actually been sampled.

Model boundaries

The ledger deposits at a constant stated rate, pauses, erodes newest surviving material top-down, and resumes. In the erosion case, whole and partial layers are removed; fossils in removed material are absent from the surviving column. No reworking is simulated.

This activity is not a basin simulator or an extinction test. It omits transport, reworking, correlation uncertainty, dating uncertainty, sampling probability, facies change, and global range data. Two local sections cannot certify global extinction.

Controls and accessibility

Pattern and color distinguish layers; each layer has a text label and a touch-sized sample control. The evidence ledger retains every observed sample. ⚙ opens presentation mode, presenter notes, and reset.
