A continuous section. Does absence mean extinction?

Does a species' last appearance in sampled rock mark where it went extinct?

Before you start. Two measurements stay separate throughout this activity: rock thickness in metres records surviving strata, and model time in Myr (one Myr is one million years) is the elapsed clock this authored model runs on. A fossil specimen marks where and when it was buried — not a full record of the organism's whole existence.

Predict

Guess before you sample: does finding where a fossil stops appearing settle whether the species went extinct there?

Try

Press sample on every surviving layer in Section A, then Show supplied age evidence to see the section's Myr chronology.

Takeaway

A local last observation in sampled rock is where the record stops, not a proven extinction moment.

Check yourself

Asteria is sampled in the lower two units of Section A but not in Gold sandstone above them. Does that alone establish that Asteria went globally extinct partway up the section?

Demo 15 of 16 · Part four · Geology · Stage 2

A missing interval, and where its fossils went.

Is a rock gap always someone's last moment, and do a gap's missing fossils end up sitting at its boundary?

Before you start. A contact is the surface between two rock units. Where rock — and therefore time — is missing across it, that missing interval is a hiatus. Section B's hiatus is produced by an authored erosion event, which deletes rock (and any fossils inside it) rather than moving it anywhere. Section A handed forward that a local record has an edge; here the edge is a gap rather than the top of the section.

Predict

Guess before you look: once you find rock missing here, will the surviving evidence also tell you why it is missing?

Try

Press Show supplied age evidence to reveal the gap, then sample Red siltstone and Cross-bedded sand on either side of it.

Takeaway

Erosion deletes rock and its fossils outright; nothing is relocated to the boundary that erosion leaves behind.

Check yourself

Erosion removed the top of Red siltstone and all of Pale limestone in Section B. Are the fossils that were once in that removed rock now sitting at today's contact, waiting to be found there?

Demo 15 of 16 · Part four · Geology · Stage 3

Two sections, one gap between them.

Does a second section that keeps depositing through a first section's gap settle what the gap means?

Before you start. Sections C and D start identically, then diverge: C has a pause (no deposition, but no erosion either — a hiatus with a different cause than Section B's), while D keeps accumulating rock through that same span. Stage 2 showed one cause of a gap; this stage's pause shows a second, equally valid one.

Predict

Guess before you compare: if Section D preserves fossils from exactly the span missing at C, does that settle the question for the species everywhere?

Try

Show supplied age evidence for both sections, then sample below C's gap and the matching Green mudstone layer in D.

Takeaway

A second section can show what a gap hides locally, but two local sections still cannot certify a global claim.

Check yourself

Section D preserves fossils from exactly the span missing at Section C. Do these two local sections, compared, prove a global claim about where the species lived?

Guidehow to investigate missing time

A rock column always has less time in it than it looks like it should. Where did the missing time go, and what can you honestly conclude from what survived?

Where this sits: this is one of ten Part-four example projects — an AI-built classroom tool whose goal is a bounded rock-record inference, its engine is a live synthetic ledger, and its assumptions are one tap away.

  1. Section A. Press sample on each surviving layer, then Show supplied age evidence to see the section's chronology.
  2. Section B. Reveal the gap, then sample the surviving rock immediately below and above it.
  3. Sections C & D. Reveal chronology for both, then sample below C's gap and the matching layer in D.
  4. In every stage, choose a warranted conclusion from the visible evidence, then reveal the authored history to compare.

Live the columns, the ledger and the evidence-readiness check

Scripted Section A's case history itself

The layer sequence, deposition rates, fossil placements and the authored reveal text are a hand-authored teaching fixture standing in for a real stratigraphic section — not a simulation that could have come out differently.

How it works

depth in a layer (m) = rate (m/Myr) × elapsed time in that layer (Myr)

Each layer deposits at a stated constant rate. A sample reveals only the specimens whose position survives; specimen ages are placed directly on the authored timeline, then mapped to a depth using that layer's own rate. Chronology anchors mark each surviving layer's base and top in Myr; the model checks that every anchor agrees with the rock-derived mapping before it will show it.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • You never derive an exact Myr value from thickness yourself. The only source of an exact time value anywhere in this activity is the supplied chronology anchors revealed by Show supplied age evidence — mirroring how real geologic ages come from independent dating evidence, not from measuring a bed and assuming a rate.
  • Deposition is constant-rate within each layer. A real bed's rate can vary; this model holds it fixed so the mapping is exact rather than approximate.
  • No dating uncertainty and no measurement error are modeled. A supplied Myr value is exact here; a real radiometric or biostratigraphic age carries a stated error bar this activity omits.

Sources

  • This stage cites no external figures: every number is either computed live from the authored ledger or is part of the hand-authored fixture itself. The activity's event/occurrence framing takes inspiration from preservation/occurrence-event teaching contexts including StratPal (see the footer); that is context, not a data source for any number shown here.
Boundary: this model does not simulate global species distribution, transport, or a real dating method. "Extinction" here is only ever a local last-appearance in one authored section, and equal rock thickness in this section's own three layers does not mean equal elapsed time — each layer's own rate decides that.
What is live, and what is fixed

The gap and its removed material are computed here; the erosion event itself is authored

Live the gap's Myr span and which fossils the erosion removed

Once you reveal Section B's chronology, the size of the gap and exactly which specimens the erosion event removed (versus which survive) are computed here from the ledger, not looked up or pre-rendered.

Scripted Section B's erosion event and its removed thickness

That an erosion event occurred, how much rock it removed, and from which layers, are fixed teaching-fixture facts — authored so this stage has a gap with a knowable, revealable cause, rather than an unexplained one.

How it works

removed thickness is subtracted top-down until the authored erosion amount is used up

The erosion event removes rock starting from the top of the section downward, deleting whole layers first and then partially eating into the next one. Any fossil whose position falls inside the removed thickness is deleted along with that rock — it is not moved to a new position.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • Erosion deletes; it does not relocate. A removed fossil is gone from the surviving column, not repositioned at the resulting contact — the Check-yourself item on this stage tests exactly this.
  • The gap's cause is authored, not inferred. In a real section you would need independent evidence to tell an erosional hiatus from a non-depositional one; here the reveal simply states which one this case is.
  • A hiatus does not require erosion in general. This case's gap happens to be erosional; Stage 3's Section C pause shows a gap with no erosion at all. Do not generalize from one case to "every gap means erosion."

Sources

  • No external figures are cited on this stage; the gap size and removed-thickness figures are computed live from the authored ledger described above.
Boundary: this model does not simulate real erosional processes, reworking, or where eroded sediment and its fossils physically go after removal — it only tracks what stays in the column and what the ledger records as deleted.
What is live, and what is fixed

The comparison is computed here; both sections' histories are fixed

Live both sections' evidence ledgers and the readiness check that requires both before a conclusion is offered

Section C's pause and Section D's continued deposition are read from each section's own ledger as you sample and reveal chronology; the requirement that you have evidence from BOTH sections before choosing a conclusion is enforced here, not merely suggested.

Scripted the paired case histories for C and D

That C pauses for exactly 2.00 Myr while D keeps depositing Green mudstone through the same span is an authored pairing, built so the comparison has a clean, revealable answer.

How it works

a pause records elapsed time with zero deposited thickness — a second, non-erosional cause of a gap

A pause event advances the model clock without adding rock, producing a hiatus with no erosion involved at all — the model's second mechanism for "missing time," distinct from Stage 2's erosion.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • Two local sections are not a global or basin-scale survey. The comparison shows what a second local section can and cannot add to a first section's gap; it is not a rule for how many sections settle a claim in general.
  • The two sections are given identical starting rock so the pause and the fossil-in-D comparison are the only variable; a real paired-section study would need to establish correlation between sections independently.
  • No sampling-density or facies-correlation limits are modeled beyond what each section's own layer list already represents.

Sources

  • No external figures are cited on this stage; the comparison is computed live from the two authored ledgers described above.
Boundary: two authored local sections are not a basin-scale or global survey. This stage shows what local absence can and cannot support when a second local section is available — it is not evidence that any two sections, in general, are enough to certify a global claim.

Gaps in the Rock Record — Teacher Guide

One HTML file, offline, no AI at runtime. You need: a screen to project or share; no other materials.

Learning goals

Not stated objectives — what the activity itself demonstrably shows, one per stage:

  1. A local last observation in sampled rock is where the record stops, not a proven extinction moment.
  2. Erosion deletes rock and its fossils outright; nothing is relocated to the boundary that erosion leaves behind.
  3. A second section can show what a gap hides locally, but two local sections still cannot certify a global claim.

Where this sits on the path

This is one of ten Part-four example projects: an AI-built classroom tool whose goal is a bounded rock-record inference, whose engine is a live synthetic ledger computed as you sample, and whose assumptions — no dating uncertainty, no global distribution model, no fossil relocation — are one tap away in Details. It does not build on a specific earlier demo in the tour; it applies the tour's own discipline (name the goal, expose the assumptions, keep the evidence available) to a rock column instead of a model.

One question for the session

How much time does the rock in front of you actually show, and how much is missing? Surviving rock and elapsed time are different measurements, and this activity never lets you compute one from the other by eye — only supplied chronology evidence fixes an exact Myr value.

The one sentence to land: a local rock record can only ever tell you where ITS evidence stops — not where the organism, the deposition, or the story actually ended.

Stage 1 — Section A

Question. Does a species' last appearance in sampled rock mark where it went extinct?

Predict prompt. Before sampling, guess: does finding where a fossil stops appearing settle whether the species went extinct there?

Activity. Sample all three layers in Section A (Blue mudstone, Green mudstone, Gold sandstone), then press Show supplied age evidence. Asteria appears in the lower two layers and nowhere above; ask the room what that absence does and does not prove before choosing a conclusion.

Expected observation. Every layer samples cleanly with no gap in this section (the evidence ledger and the linked time view both confirm a continuous 6 Myr record); the "warranted" conclusion is available only after middle and top are sampled and the chronology is shown.

Check-yourself answer key. "Does Asteria's absence above Blue and Green mudstone settle global extinction?" — No: a local last observation is not a global extinction record.

Teaching limit. This section has no missing interval at all; it is here to establish the extinction-inference limit before Stage 2 adds a genuine gap.

Stage 2 — Section B

Question. Is a rock gap always someone's last moment, and do a gap's missing fossils end up sitting at its boundary?

Predict prompt. Before looking, guess: once you find rock missing here, will the surviving evidence also tell you why it is missing?

Activity. Press Show supplied age evidence first so the gap is visible, then sample Red siltstone (below) and Cross-bedded sand (above). Reveal the authored history and have the room compare "what the gap alone showed" against "what erosion actually did."

Expected observation. The reveal states the erosion event removed 15.00 m of Pale limestone entirely and 10.00 m from the top of Red siltstone, taking their fossils with it — nothing from the removed rock reappears at today's contact.

Check-yourself answer key. "Are the removed fossils now sitting at the contact?" — No: erosion deletes rock and fossils; nothing relocates to the boundary.

Teaching limit. This section's gap happens to be erosional; a gap does not require erosion in general (Section C's pause in Stage 3 has none at all). Do not let the room generalize "every gap is erosion" from this one case.

Stage 3 — Sections C and D

Question. Does a second section that keeps depositing through a first section's gap settle what the gap means?

Predict prompt. Before comparing, guess: if Section D preserves fossils from exactly the span missing at C, does that settle the question for the species everywhere?

Activity. Reveal supplied chronology for both C and D, then sample below C's gap and the matching Green mudstone layer in D. The evidence-readiness check requires samples and chronology from BOTH sections before offering a conclusion — point this out as a deliberate gate, not a bug.

Expected observation. D's Green mudstone preserves a fossil from exactly the 2.00 Myr span that C's pause leaves blank; the room should see this as "D fills in what C cannot show," not as proof that the species was present everywhere during that span.

Check-yourself answer key. "Do C and D together prove a global claim?" — No: two local sections are not a global sample, however informative.

Teaching limit. The two sections are given identical starting rock so the pause is the only variable; a real paired-section study would need to establish correlation between sections independently, which this activity does not model.

Misconceptions

"Once a fossil stops appearing, the organism is extinct." No. A local last observation is where a section's own evidence stops, not where the organism itself ended everywhere (Stage 1).
"Equal rock thickness means equal elapsed time." No. Section A's own three layers deposit at different rates (10, 7 and 8 m/Myr) over the identical 2 Myr each, so they come out different thicknesses for the same elapsed time (Stage 1).
"A rock gap always means erosion happened." No. Section B's gap is erosional, but Section C's pause in Stage 3 is a gap with no erosion at all — non-deposition is a separate, equally valid cause (Stages 2–3).
"Two sections that compare well together certify a global claim." No. Comparing Sections C and D is more evidence than either alone, but it is still two named local places, not a global or basin-scale survey (Stage 3).

Discussion questions

  • Section A's three layers each took 2 Myr to deposit but came out different thicknesses. What would go wrong if you tried to read elapsed time directly off a ruler held against the rock?
  • Section B's contact would look the same on the surviving rock whether the missing time came from erosion or from a long pause. What additional, real-world evidence would let a geologist tell those two apart, that this activity does not model?
  • If Section D's rock at the matching interval had ALSO been missing, would that change what you could conclude about Section C's gap? Why or why not?

Questions you will get

“Doesn’t the app already know the real history? Why hide it?”

It does — every case has an authored reveal. The point of hiding it until a conclusion is chosen is to force the claim to rest on the VISIBLE evidence, not on knowing the answer key. That is also why choosing a conclusion is not scored: it is feedback on the claim, not a test of memorised history.

“Why can’t I just measure the rock and compute the missing time myself?”

Because that is the specific habit this activity is built to interrupt. The only source of an exact Myr value anywhere on screen is Show supplied age evidence — a stand-in for independent chronological evidence (radiometric dates, correlated marker beds) that a real geologist would need. Nothing here lets you derive an exact age from thickness and an assumed rate.

“Is this how real geologic gaps work?”

Only in outline. This is a small authored teaching fixture: it omits reworking, transport, dating uncertainty, sampling statistics, facies correlation and any global distribution model. Each stage's Details drawer states its own boundary plainly — open one in front of the room if this question comes up.
