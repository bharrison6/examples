Does one exact assay point already tell you which inhibition pattern you are looking at?

Before you start. An enzyme's initial rate is how fast it converts substrate before conditions change; Km is the substrate concentration where the rate reaches half of Vmax, the enzyme's fastest possible rate. Candidate model

All plotted candidates use v = Vmax·S / (a·Km + b·S). Competitive: a=3, b=1. Uncompetitive: a=1, b=3. Pure noncompetitive: a=b=3.

Teaching boundary: the competitive/uncompetitive tie at S=Km is deliberately produced by this matched-factor synthetic set. It is not a general diagnostic rule for real inhibition.

These are simplified steady-state initial-rate patterns. They omit mixed, partial, allosteric, tight-binding, time-dependent, and other inhibition behavior.

Guidehow to use this demo

Can one exact measurement always tell you which of several causes is at work?

Where this sits: an AI-built classroom tool from Part four. Its goal is to show that exact evidence can still leave two causes tied, its engine is scripted (an idealized synthetic assay model), and its assumptions are one tap away in Details.

  1. Bench. Choose a substrate concentration and press Run selected assay to record up to four exact rates, and read them against the three candidate curves.
  2. Explain. Choose the statement the evidence on your bench actually supports before the mystery pattern is revealed.
  3. Transfer. Pick the next measurement that would actually separate the remaining candidates.

Getting around

The three tabs switch stages. Details opens the formula, assumptions, and sources for the current stage. Guide reopens this card; Settings holds Open Presenter Notes, Presentation mode, and Reset.

Time

About 8 to 10 minutes for all three stages.

DetailsBench

What is live, and what is scripted

The chart and the ledger are computed from a hand-built formula, not real assay data

Scripted the calibrated baseline and the three candidate curves

Every curve on the chart is generated from v = Vmax·S / (a·Km + b·S) with fixed teaching factors; nothing here is read from an instrument or drawn from a real assay run.

Scripted every recorded assay rate

Pressing Run selected assay computes the mystery sample's rate at your chosen concentration from the same fixed formula, using whichever of the three candidate factor pairs this sample's hidden pattern uses. It is not a noisy or randomized reading — the same concentration always returns the same exact number for a given hidden pattern.

How it works

v = Vmax·S / (a·Km + b·S)

Vmax = 100 rate units, Km = 2 concentration units. Baseline (no inhibitor) uses a=b=1. Competitive: a=3, b=1. Uncompetitive: a=1, b=3. Pure noncompetitive: a=b=3. The hidden pattern is fixed when a mystery sample is drawn and does not change while you investigate it.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • No noise. Every observation is exact; there is no measurement error, curve-fitting, or hidden tolerance anywhere in the model.
  • A four-assay budget is a teaching constraint, not a real assay's practical limit.
  • Steady-state initial-rate approximation only — this is not a full time-course or progress-curve model.
  • Only six substrate concentrations (0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 20 teaching units) are offered, chosen so the S=Km tie and its resolution are both reachable within the budget.

Sources

  • Strelow et al., Mechanism of Action Assays for Enzymes, NIH Assay Guidance Manual — idealized initial-rate inhibition-pattern context and the value of measurements above Km.
Boundary: this bench does not identify a real inhibitor's molecular binding site or mechanism, and it produces no assay noise a real instrument would show. It is a bounded, exact teaching model of three idealized rate patterns.

Live whether your explanation matches the evidence

The moment you choose an explanation, this page compares it against the assays you have actually recorded, using the same rule every time: an explanation is sound only if it names exactly what the current evidence supports (a single isolated pattern, or genuine ambiguity).

Scripted the three candidate formulas and their factors

Competitive (a=3, b=1), uncompetitive (a=1, b=3), and pure noncompetitive (a=b=3) are fixed, hand-chosen factors — not fitted to data and not representative of any specific real compound.

How it works

A candidate is compatible with your evidence if it predicts every recorded rate within floating- point tolerance. If exactly one candidate remains compatible, the evidence is scored "discriminated"; if more than one remains, it is scored "ambiguous". Your explanation is sound only if it names the matching state.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • The tie at S=Km is deliberate, not a coincidence this page discovered — competitive and uncompetitive were built with factors chosen to coincide exactly at one point.
  • "Insufficient evidence" is a first-class correct answer whenever more than one candidate remains compatible; the model never rewards a forced guess.

Sources

  • Strelow et al., Mechanism of Action Assays for Enzymes, NIH Assay Guidance Manual — the same initial-rate assay context cited in Stage 1.
Boundary: reaching "insufficient evidence" is a correct scientific conclusion in this exact model. It is never treated as a wrong answer, and no real molecular mechanism is ever named or confirmed by this stage.

Live which candidates this measurement would eliminate

Pressing a measurement option computes, on the spot, whether the two remaining candidates' predicted rates at that concentration are distinct, and reports how many of the two remaining candidates that measurement would rule out.

Scripted the competitive and uncompetitive formulas

The tie this stage transfers from — both predicting 25.0 at S=Km — comes from the same fixed a=3,b=1 and a=1,b=3 factors used throughout, not from a new scenario.

How it works

Any substrate concentration other than S=Km produces two different predicted rates for the competitive and uncompetitive formulas, because the formulas differ everywhere except at their engineered crossing point. The model reports the eliminated-candidate count; it assigns no extra credit for a larger predicted gap between the two.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • The model does not rank informative measurements by predicted separation size. With zero noise, any measurable difference is equally decisive — a real, noisy assay would instead prefer the larger gap, which this idealized model deliberately does not model.
  • Only two candidates remain in play at this stage (competitive and uncompetitive); the transfer scenario does not reconsider pure noncompetitive, which the stage-2 evidence already excluded at S=Km (16.7, not 25.0).

Sources

  • Strelow et al., Mechanism of Action Assays for Enzymes, NIH Assay Guidance Manual — the same initial-rate assay context cited in Stages 1 and 2.
Boundary: this stage does not claim that measurement choice is unimportant in a real assay — only that this exact, noiseless model has no error margin for a bigger predicted gap to overcome. A real, noisy experiment would have reason to prefer it.
