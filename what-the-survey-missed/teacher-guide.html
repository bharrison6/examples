What the Survey Missed

Teacher / presenter guide · a bounded synthetic occupancy investigation

Purpose

Students separate four quantities that are commonly conflated: an observed fraction ever detected, a realized occupied-site fraction in one synthetic scenario, a generating occupancy probability (ψ), and a detection probability (p). Occupancy here means whether a site is used; it is not abundance or a number of animals.

Central lesson: fewer detections do not by themselves establish lower occupancy. Detection probability can differ between habitats.

What students do

  1. Run a fixed first survey: 24 A sites + 24 B sites = 48 site-visits.
  2. Inspect visible site histories and state what a detection difference can—and cannot—mean.
  3. After that evidence claim, optionally repeat the same fixed survey for rounds 2 and 3.
  4. Reveal the synthetic occupancy map and parameters; arrays then distinguish detections, occupied-but-missed sites, and unoccupied sites.

Clicks only inspect fixed records. They do not allocate more sampling to any site.

Declared synthetic model

Habitat A

ψ = 0.8; p = 0.25

A site is first assigned occupied/unoccupied from ψ. If occupied, each visit independently records a detection with probability p.

Habitat B

ψ = 0.5; p = 0.8

The same fixed process applies. Unoccupied sites have no detections, so the model has no false positives.

Assumptions: closure across the three visits; conditional independence of detections given occupancy; no false positives; and p constant within a habitat. They are classroom assumptions, not assertions about real local wildlife. The scenarios are seeded and synthetic.

Why the expected observation can mislead

For K visits, the expected fraction ever detected is ψ × [1 − (1 − p)K].

Do not turn the ranking into a success condition. Students should explain that observed detections combine occupancy and detectability. The evidence view shows the observed fraction; reveal separately shows realized occupancy and the generating ψ/p.

Suggested facilitation

  1. Before round 1: “What would a non-detection mean here?” Invite the distinction between “not detected” and “not present.”
  2. After round 1: ask learners to identify the unit of effort: 48 site-visits, not 48 animals counted.
  3. Before a repeat: “What changes when we revisit every site? What does not change?” The site set stays fixed; the accumulated histories gain evidence.
  4. Before reveal: require the cautious claim provided in the app. Then compare observed fractions, realized occupancy, ψ, and p by name.

Optional likelihood view

The view groups the observed K-visit histories from one habitat and evaluates a bounded grid of ψ and p values. For a specific history with d detections:

If d > 0: P(history | ψ,p) = ψ pd(1−p)K−d.

If d = 0: P(000…0 | ψ,p) = (1−ψ) + ψ(1−p)K.

Multiply the exact history probabilities over sites (or sum their log likelihoods). Do not add a binomial coefficient to a particular ordered history. The displayed shading is relative likelihood after scaling to the grid maximum. It is not a posterior probability and not a calibrated 95% confidence interval.

One-visit ambiguity: with one visit, detections chiefly identify the product ψp. Many combinations of a high ψ/low p and low ψ/high p can fit the same observed fraction. This is why the app shows ambiguity rather than a unique occupancy estimate.

Interpretation boundaries

  • The model teaches a mechanism of detection confounding; it does not estimate a real species, habitat, population, or conservation priority.
  • The “truth” is the current scenario’s generated state. It is useful for checking the teaching model, not a hidden fact that field surveys disclose.
  • Three rounds are intentionally finite. A correct scientific conclusion can remain uncertain.
  • The transfer response should name an alternative detection process, such as harder-to-detect animals at one habitat, rather than treating detections as direct counts.

Presenter controls and reproducibility

? reopens instructions. ⚙ contains presentation mode and notes. Reset responses clears learner responses while retaining the current seed. Replay as practice begins a fresh attempt with the same fixed sites and records; it is not a blind new test once truth has been seen. New seeded scenario produces a different synthetic scenario. After truth is revealed, later rounds are visibly labelled explore mode. The activity uses no network, external asset, or runtime AI service.
