Can fewer sightings mean fewer occupied sites?

Survey two comparable synthetic habitat groups. Each round visits every site. Your evidence is a detection history, not a count of animals.

Occupancy means whether a site is used in this model. It is not abundance, the number of animals.

Presenter notes

First ask what the detection difference means. Do not reward the raw ranking: expected detection can favor B even though A has greater occupancy probability.

How to use this investigation

  1. Run a first round across all 48 fixed sites.
  2. Inspect detection histories, then state what a detection difference can and cannot mean.
  3. Choose whether a second or third fixed round helps.
  4. Reveal the synthetic map only after the evidence claim.

Assumptions: closure across visits; conditionally independent detections given occupancy; no false positives; and a constant p within each habitat. These are teaching assumptions, not local wildlife claims.
