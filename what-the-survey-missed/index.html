What the Survey Missed

Teacher and presenter guide - bounded synthetic occupancy investigation - Murray State University - Bryant Harrison

Purpose and learning target

Students distinguish observed fraction ever detected, realized occupied-site fraction in one synthetic scenario, occupancy probability (psi), and detection probability (p). Occupancy means whether a site is used in this model; it is not abundance or a number of animals.

Target claim: fewer detections do not by themselves establish lower occupancy. Detection probability can differ between habitats.

Every round visits the same 24 Habitat A and 24 Habitat B sites: 48 site-visits. A site click inspects a fixed detection history; it never changes the sample.

Current app flow

  1. Open index.html locally; the Guide overlay opens automatically. Dismiss it, then run fixed round 1 across all 48 sites.
  2. Inspect observed fraction ever detected and choose the cautious claim: a difference can reflect occupancy, detection probability, or both.
  3. Only then choose fixed rounds 2 and 3, transfer, optional likelihood, or reveal.
  4. After reveal, the same arrays distinguish detections, occupied-but-missed sites, and unoccupied sites. Further rounds are exploration, not a blind new test.

Sites remain occupied or unoccupied across the three visits (closure). Given occupancy, detections are conditionally independent; there are no false positives; and p is constant within a habitat. These are teaching assumptions, not claims about local wildlife.

Why raw detections can mislead

For K visits, expected fraction ever detected is psi x [1 - (1 - p)^K].

10.200.40B is expected to have more detections although A has greater psi.
30.46250.496More visits improve evidence but do not guarantee a finite ranking matches occupancy.
Do not score the ranking. After reveal, compare observed fraction, realized occupancy, generating psi, and p by name. A seeded realization can differ from either expectation.

Optional likelihood view

The app uses observed K-visit histories for one habitat only. For an ordered history with d detections, likelihood is psi p^d (1-p)^(K-d) when d is positive; for an all-zero history it is (1-psi) + psi(1-p)^K.

Interpret carefully: shading is relative likelihood on a bounded psi/p grid. It is not posterior probability or a calibrated confidence interval. Multiply exact ordered-history probabilities over sites; do not add a binomial coefficient to one particular history.

With one visit, detections chiefly identify psi p. A high-psi/low-p and lower-psi/higher-p combination can fit the same observed fraction. The app supplies axis labels plus keyboard/touch readout rather than a unique occupancy estimate.

Facilitation and boundaries

  • Before round 1, contrast “not detected” with “not present.”
  • After round 1, ask for the unit of effort: 48 site-visits, not 48 animals counted.
  • Before another round, ask what changes (histories) and what stays fixed (sites).
  • For transfer, ask whether a different detection process could explain fewer detections.

This activity teaches detection confounding. It does not estimate a real species, habitat, population, conservation priority, or field-survey result.

How to run this investigation.

  1. Run a first round across all 48 fixed sites.
  2. Inspect detection histories, then state what a detection difference can and cannot mean.
  3. Choose whether a second or third fixed round helps.
  4. Reveal the synthetic map only after the evidence claim.

Assumptions: closure across visits; conditionally independent detections given occupancy; no false positives; and a constant p within each habitat. These are teaching assumptions, not local wildlife claims.
