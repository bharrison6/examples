🧠Zero to Unbeatable

One tic-tac-toe board, three real mechanisms. 1a exposes eight hand-written rules and checks the resulting policy. 1b learns a value table in visible game bursts. 1c visibly runs a fresh copy of the 1b algorithm for 20,000 seeded games, freezes its current estimates as examples, then trains shared neural-network weights. It reports positions kept out of training separately from a reproducible play comparison. Ultimate tic-tac-toe remains an optional advanced extension.

How the terms relate
Artificial intelligence

A broad label for machines performing tasks that call for judgment, prediction, planning, perception, or language.

Machine learning

A part of AI in which behavior improves from data or experience. Neural networks are one family of machine-learning models.

Generative AI

Systems that produce new text, images, audio, or other content. Modern examples often use neural networks, but “generative” describes what a system does.

Models and agents

A language model predicts sequences. Reasoning training can improve multi-step work; an agent adds tools, memory, and a loop around a model.

These labels overlap; they are not one universal ladder.

Rules-based systems, learned tables, neural networks, generative systems, reasoning methods, and agents describe different mechanisms or behaviors. Ask what was learned, what was written by people, and what the system can actually do.

Three ideas to carry forward

  1. Ask what kind of system you are looking at. Human-written rules, learned values, neural networks, language models, and agents behave differently.
  2. Match the tool to the job and the review you can provide. Choose a workflow whose results you can inspect.
  3. Bring your discipline’s standards. A useful classroom tool starts from the learning goal, exposes its assumptions, and makes evidence available for challenge.
