When something is labelled “AI”, ask which ring. Demos 1, 2 and 3 are the same game played by three different machines, one per ring. Most of the AI already running in industry is nowhere near the gold chips in the middle — and the outer rings did not stop working when the inner ones arrived.

Part one

What AI is

Four rungs, working outside-in through those rings. The first three are the same game — tic-tac-toe — played by three completely different kinds of machine, so the only thing changing is the machinery. Then the one inside the chatbot.

Leave with: AI is a family of methods. Each ring solves the problem the ring outside it could not, and each one is still in production somewhere today — the outer rings did not go away when the inner ones arrived.

Demos 1, 2 and 3 are one demo — the same game against three different kinds of machine, switched at the top of it. Demo 4 works the same way, walking its three layers.

DEMO 1Rules — the outermost ring

A hand-written ladder of if-statements: take the win, block the row, take the fork. The rule that fired is highlighted on every move, so you can read the whole mind of it. Most people would call this AI.

A person wrote every line of that. The machine worked nothing out.

the same idea, in the wild Deep Blue beat Garry Kasparov 3½–2½ in 1997 on hand-tuned rules and search. R1/XCON configured VAX orders at Digital from 1980 and kicked off the expert-systems boom. And the traffic signals outside — and the PLC in part three — still run exactly this way.
DEMO 2Machine learning — one ring in

Same board. An opponent that has played zero games and holds no rules at all, learning only from won, lost or drew. Train it in three-second bursts until you cannot win — then the app proves it, playing out all 15,776 games still reachable against it. Tic-tac-toe has 255,168 games in total across 5,478 positions, so a laptop really can check every one. That is why this is a proof and not a win record.

Same result as the rules. Nobody wrote a single one of them.

the same idea, in the wild Your spam filter, card-fraud detection, and the recommendation engines behind most of what gets shown to you. None of it is a neural network, and none of it writes.
DEMO 3Neural networks — and the wall that made them necessary

Ultimate tic-tac-toe, in the same app: nine boards, and the square you play decides which board your opponent must play in next. The same learner comes apart here, because the shortcut it relied on — score each board on its own and add up — is simply wrong once the boards are coupled.

A table can only look up what it has already stored. This is the wall neural networks exist to get over: computing a value for a position you have never seen.

the same idea, in the wild AlexNet won ImageNet in 2012 and restarted the whole field. AlphaGo beat Lee Sedol four games to one in 2016. AlphaFold predicts protein structures — see part two. Also: your phone finding photos of your dog, and every voice-to-text you have used.
Machine learning · not generative AI

🧠Zero to Unbeatable

Three opponents on one tic-tac-toe board. First, eight if/else rules a person wrote out — it has played zero games, it tells you which rule it just used, and you cannot beat it. Then a learner that starts from nothing and reaches the same unbeatable play after ~20,000 games. Then nine boards at once, where neither approach scales. Same exhaustive search proves both of the first two.

DEMO 4Generative AI — the innermost ring

A real transformer trains from scratch in front of the room — gibberish to words to sentences. Then a reasoning model is built out of it, and an agent out of that: the three gold chips in the picture above, in that order.

A base model predicts. Reasoning and agents are layers bolted on top.

the same idea, in the wild ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are the LLM chip. The “thinking” modes are the reasoning chip. Coding assistants that run commands and read files are the agent chip — and the demos you are reading about were built with them.

Finale

Five things to take back

  1. “AI” is a set of rings, not a synonym for ChatGPT. The tic-tac-toe learner is real AI with no language in it at all. When something is labelled AI, ask which ring.
  2. Generative AI is not magic. It is a predictor trained on data; reasoning models and agents are layers built on top. You watched every layer get built in Glass Box.
  3. The answer is only as good as the ask. Context, a success spec and the domain’s own vocabulary are free performance. Bad answer? Ask which lever you left unpulled.
  4. The curve is steeper than you drew it — and this field has crashed before. Both are true at once, and holding both is what separates a judgement from a guess.
  5. The opportunity is in your discipline. Pair AI with your subject expertise and you get tools like the five in part three — tailored, classroom-ready, and fast to build.
